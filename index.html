Zooplankton Diel Vertical Migration

Each night Daphnia rise toward the surface to feed, and each morning they sink to hide from fish in the dark. These drawings run a dynamic fitness model of that daily migration for Trout Lake, Wisconsin.

Drawings
  1. 01 Live Buoy Data Driven by real-time temperature and light readings from the Trout Lake buoy, updated every few minutes.
  2. 02 Lake Through Time Plays more than four decades of Trout Lake field data, 1981 to 2025, one week per day-night cycle. In winter the ice comes in on the real dates. A slider lets you go back to any month.
  3. 03 Lake in Sound The same decades played on piano. Each note is one Daphnia: shallower sounds higher, larger sounds louder, and more Daphnia means more notes. Jazz-voiced C major 13 by day drifts to F major 13 at night, and the piano is muffled under ice. Turn your sound on.
