Sources

The model and every dataset behind the drawings, with what each one is used for. The long-term datasets come from the North Temperate Lakes Long-Term Ecological Research program (NTL-LTER), run by the UW–Madison Center for Limnology at Trout Lake Station.

Model

DaphniaDVM

McAfee, B. J. 2021. Programming Simulations of Diel Vertical Migration Behavior of Zooplankton. Lawrence University Honors Projects 157. https://lux.lawrence.edu/luhp/157

The model behind all three drawings, ported to JavaScript with the author's permission. Builds on De Stasio et al. (1993) and the dynamic programming approach of Mangel & Clark (1988). As in the original, each size class is spread around its optimal depth as a normal distribution with a standard deviation of 3.84 m. Its May 1992 Trout Lake example profile supplies the fish layer for years before sonar surveys began.

Live data

Trout Lake buoy

UW–Madison Center for Limnology. Trout Lake Buoy Live Data. https://uwcfl.github.io/buoy-viewer/trout.html

Live Buoy Data: water temperature from 0 to 30 m, wind and air temperature, refreshed about every 10 minutes. Real-time data, not quality controlled.

Long-term Trout Lake data (NTL-LTER, Environmental Data Initiative)

Zooplankton

NTL LTER, E.H. Stanley, S.R. Carpenter, and J.J. Magnuson. 2026. North Temperate Lakes LTER: Zooplankton – Trout Lake Area 1982 – current ver 42. Environmental Data Initiative.

Daphnia density (all species, 402 sample dates, 1981–2025) sets how many dots and notes there are; measured body lengths set the size mix. Each dot stands for about 1.2 billion Daphnia.

Water temperature

Magnuson, J.J., S.R. Carpenter, and E.H. Stanley. 2026. North Temperate Lakes LTER: Physical Limnology of Primary Study Lakes 1981 – current ver 38. Environmental Data Initiative. https://doi.org/10.6073/pasta/eeaa2a029eda1683f004d06da5d8d808 (Accessed 2026-10-09).

Temperature by depth, week by week, for Lake Through Time and Lake in Sound.

Chlorophyll

Magnuson, J.J., S.R. Carpenter, and E.H. Stanley. 2025. North Temperate Lakes LTER: Chlorophyll – Trout Lake Area 1981 – current ver 33. Environmental Data Initiative. https://doi.org/10.6073/pasta/659e43f4796f71e3a37673a0f2d7a77a (Accessed 2026-10-09).

Food for the Daphnia. Median chlorophyll by depth for the current month in Live Buoy Data; week-by-week profiles in Lake Through Time and Lake in Sound.

Fish

Magnuson, J., S. Carpenter, and E. Stanley. 2025. North Temperate Lakes LTER: Pelagic Prey – Sonar Data 2001 – current ver 36. Environmental Data Initiative. https://doi.org/10.6073/pasta/7ba35a0a5f23fd0fcc7039187a0fbd91 (Accessed 2026-10-09).

Zooplankton-eating fish per 1,000 m³ by depth (the model's predator units), from the Interval Density table: Trout Lake south basin, one midsummer night survey per year. The 2020–2024 average for Live Buoy Data; each year's own survey for Lake Through Time and Lake in Sound.

Ice

Magnuson, J.J., S.R. Carpenter, and E.H. Stanley. 2026. North Temperate Lakes LTER: Ice Duration – Trout Lake Area 1981 – current ver 32. Environmental Data Initiative. https://doi.org/10.6073/pasta/d2c65ae81f95dd29cc6568344621680c (Accessed 2026-10-09).

Ice-on and ice-off dates for each winter in the timeline drawings.

Wind and sunlight

Magnuson, J.J., S.R. Carpenter, and E.H. Stanley. 2026. North Temperate Lakes LTER: Meteorological Data – Woodruff Airport 1989 – current ver 41. Environmental Data Initiative. https://doi.org/10.6073/pasta/ef5fbb5faa65e57449de0faeff8b497f (Accessed 2026-10-09).

Hourly sunlight (PAR) and wind for the timeline drawings. Before 1989, light is estimated from the sun's position and wind is typical for the season.
